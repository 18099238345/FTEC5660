# Homework 1 Reflection

Two AI-related developments from the past ten days changed how I think about
both the opportunities and the responsibilities in my future career. On 18
September 2026, Anthropic announced a partnership with Accenture to embed
independent evaluators inside a frontier AI lab. These evaluators are expected
to observe model development more closely, conduct red-team and alignment
assessments, and identify blind spots. On 15 September 2026, Google described
how its AI systems are being applied to areas such as health, disaster
resilience, education, accessibility, and economic opportunity. Taken together,
the two events show that AI is moving in two directions at the same time: it is
becoming more useful in high-impact real-world settings, while the need for
credible evaluation and accountability is becoming more urgent.

Before reading about these developments, I mainly thought of an AI career in
terms of building models or applications with higher accuracy and more useful
features. I now see that deployment quality depends on much more than model
performance. In business and FinTech, an AI system can affect financial
decisions, customer data, compliance, and public trust. A technically impressive
agent is not valuable if its decisions cannot be tested, monitored, explained,
and challenged. Anthropic's embedded-evaluation proposal therefore made AI
assurance feel like a core engineering function rather than an external check
performed after a product is finished. Google's examples also reminded me that
domain knowledge matters: meaningful impact comes from understanding the people,
institutions, and risks surrounding a problem, not simply connecting a powerful
model to an interface.

This changes my career plan in a practical way. I still want to learn how to
build agentic AI systems, but I also want to develop skills in evaluation,
data governance, security, and model-risk management. In the short term, I plan
to treat testing as part of system design by creating measurable success
criteria, checking outputs on unseen data, documenting failure cases, and
keeping humans responsible for consequential decisions. In the longer term, I
would like to work at the intersection of AI engineering and responsible
deployment, especially in a regulated field such as financial services. My goal
is no longer only to make AI capable of completing a task; it is to make the
result reliable enough that users and organizations have good reasons to trust
it.
